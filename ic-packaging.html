IC Packaging and Assembly

Semiconductor packaging and assembly, from established formats to wafer level.

Packaging takes a finished die and turns it into something that can be handled, soldered, and cooled. It governs how heat escapes, how the electrical connection behaves at speed, and how large the finished part is. Established leaded formats remain common where board space is not critical, while leadless and ball grid array formats dominate where density matters.

Wafer level packaging pushes this furthest by forming connections across the wafer before singulation, producing parts close to die size. The trade is a more demanding board assembly process and tighter tolerances downstream.

What buyers usually specify

  • Package format and pin count, and whether it is leaded, leadless, or ball grid array
  • Thermal path and power dissipation requirements
  • Electrical performance at the intended operating frequency
  • Substrate technology and interconnect approach
  • Test and burn in coverage offered alongside assembly
