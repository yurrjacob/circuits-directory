Motion Control ICs

Motor drivers, stepper and brushless controllers, and the gate drive stage between logic and power.

Motion control silicon sits between a controller and a motor. Integrated driver ICs combine control logic and output stage in one device, which suits lower power applications and keeps the design compact. At higher currents the output stage moves to discrete transistors, and a dedicated gate driver handles the timing and level shifting between them.

Motor type largely determines the device. Stepper drivers deal with microstepping and current regulation. Brushless drivers deal with commutation, which may be sensored or derived from back electromotive force, and increasingly include field oriented control.

What buyers usually specify

  • Motor type: brushed, stepper, brushless, or induction
  • Supply voltage and continuous current, with peak current headroom
  • Integrated driver or separate gate driver with external transistors
  • Control interface: step and direction, PWM, or a serial protocol
  • Protection: overcurrent, overtemperature, and stall detection
